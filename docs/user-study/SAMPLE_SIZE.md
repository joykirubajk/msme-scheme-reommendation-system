# Sample size and honest reporting

## How many participants

| Goal | Minimum | Better | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Find the main usability problems | 5 | 5 per user group | Nielsen & Landauer (1993): about 5 users uncover most problems in a qualitative test; more users mostly find the same problems again. |
| Report a SUS score | 8 | 12-15 | Tullis & Stetson (2004): SUS becomes reasonably stable around 12 participants. Below that, report it as indicative only. |
| Compare two groups (e.g. MSME owners vs students) | - | 10+ per group | Smaller groups cannot support a comparison. Do not claim a difference with fewer. |

**Recommended for this project: 8-12 participants**, ideally including at least 3-5 people who actually run or plan to run a small business. If you can only reach students, say so in the report.

## How to report

- Always give **n** next to every number: "Mean SUS 72.5 (SD 9.8, n = 10)".
- Report **mean and standard deviation** for SUS, ratings and time on task; for time also give the **median**, because a single slow participant skews the mean.
- Report task success as a count and percentage: "9 of 10 participants (90%) completed Task 4 without help."
- Describe the participants: how many, how recruited, business owners or not, age range. No names.
- Interpret SUS against the usual benchmark: about 68 is average (Sauro 2011). Say "above / below the commonly cited average of 68", not "excellent", unless the score is clearly high (80+).
- **Do not** claim statistical significance, generalise to "Indian MSMEs", or turn a small sample into percentages of "users" without n.
- Report problems you saw even if few people hit them, with how many did.
- If the ranking service was down in any session, say which sessions; their relevance ratings are about the fallback ranking.
